Korea uses 220 V, 60 Hz electricity. Check that your devices support this voltage and frequency.

Korea uses Type F plugs, the same type as Germany’s [Schuko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schuko) standard.
However, unlike in Germany, a [Europlug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europlug) may fit loosely in a Korean socket, causing a poor electrical connection or potentially damaging equipment. We recommend bringing a travel adapter suitable for Korean sockets.

## Geography
### Weather
Winters in Korea can be harsh. In late November, when the event takes place, temperatures can fall below freezing and humidity is very low.

Indoor spaces, on the other hand, are often heated strongly. We recommend wearing light layers with a heavy coat over them.

### Terrain
Gangnam, where the venue is located, may look like a flat grid on a map, but it is actually quite hilly.
This can make it difficult to reach the venue by shared bicycle.

Much of Seoul is hilly, not just Gangnam. For people who enjoy hiking, however, this can be an advantage.
Seoul is one of the few cities with tall mountains suitable for hiking close to the city center.
The city also operates facilities where visitors can rent hiking equipment. For details, visit the [Seoul Tourism Organization website](https://english.visitseoul.net/seoul-hiking).

## Payments
Many restaurants and shops in Korea use self-service kiosks that only accept credit cards, and many of these reject cards issued outside Korea.

If your payment fails at a kiosk, or if you want to pay in cash, ask a staff member at the counter to process your payment.

Street food vendors usually do not accept credit cards. Pay in cash.

## Emergency Contacts
In an emergency in Korea, use the following numbers. You can call or send a text message.

* Police: 112
* Fire and ambulance: 119
